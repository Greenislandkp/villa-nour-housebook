Guest Registration (TM30)

The TM30 is a Thai immigration form that every property owner is required to file, reporting where foreign visitors are staying during their time in Thailand — it's simply part of local regulations, nothing to worry about.

Please send your host a copy of the passport for everyone staying overnight at the villa — this is required for us to complete the registration and meet our legal obligations, so we do need this from each guest.

Getting Around

Transport

Getting here by ferryThere's no airport on Koh Phangan, so you'll arrive by ferry — mainly via Thong Sala Pier (the main terminal, on the west coast, served by Lomprayah, Seatran Discovery, Raja Ferry and Songserm from Koh Samui, Surat Thani and Koh Tao) or Haad Rin Pier on the east coast. From either pier, shared songthaews wait for every ferry and leave once full, dropping passengers off one by one (fixed price, roughly 150–200 THB depending on distance). Private taxis are also available right at the pier for a direct transfer — more expensive (roughly 300–500 THB), but faster. There are no meters, so agree on the price before getting in.
Songthaew / taxiThere's no Grab, Uber or public bus on the island. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) can be flagged down from the roadside or found at Thong Sala Pier and major markets — just raise your hand. Motorbike taxis wear vests marked "TAXI". There are no meters, so always agree on the fare with the driver before you start the ride.
Private taxiRelatively expensive since there's no Uber or Grab on the island — most of the time you'll need to book in advance. See contacts ↓
Scooter rentalA valid driving licence is required, a helmet is compulsory, and shops usually keep your passport or a cash deposit until the bike is returned. Three-wheeled scooters are also available for rent on the island — please be aware that they're not officially permitted on the road, even though they're commonly tolerated. In case of an accident, insurance may not cover you, as these are modified three-wheeled vehicles. See contacts ↓
Car rentalUseful for larger groups or day trips further across the island. See agencies ↓

Good to Know

Things to Know

Power Cuts

Power cuts happen from time to time — it's part of island life. You may also occasionally notice power fluctuations, which can affect the lights or A/C. These usually don't last long, and this is outside of our control. We provide flashlights, but there's no generator on site.

If a power cut or fluctuation lasts more than an hour, please check the fuse box shown to you during check-in. If it still hasn't resolved after an hour, let us know so we can look into it and check with the local authorities in case of a wider issue on the power lines.

Local Wildlife

As with any tropical environment, you may come across local wildlife such as ants, mosquitoes, spiders and geckos. We maintain high cleaning and pest-control standards and will happily share tips to help keep nature's little visitors outside where they belong.

Mosquitoes are most active around sunset, for about an hour — mosquito coils are provided at the villa to help keep them away.

Between March and July, after heavy monsoon rain, you may occasionally see swarms of small flying insects known locally as "malaeng mao" (flying termites) appear at nightfall — sometimes in the hundreds. They're completely harmless, but they're drawn to bright light. If this happens, simply turn off the lights inside and leave an outside light on instead — the insects will head there and move on within a little while.

Getting Around the Island

There's no Grab, Uber or similar app on Koh Phangan, so private taxis can be relatively expensive — this is why most visitors choose to rent a scooter or a car to get around. See the Transport section for rental contacts and songthaew (shared taxi) info.

If you're not comfortable riding a scooter, it's best to stick to public songthaews or private taxis instead — there's no shame in it, and it's by far the safest choice.

Road Safety

If you're planning to ride a scooter during your stay, please always wear a helmet and make sure you're carrying a valid driving licence (ideally an international driving permit, or a Thai licence) — this is required by law and is also usually a condition of your travel insurance in case of an accident.

Please take extra care on the roads: some areas of the island are under construction, and stretches of road can be sandy, uneven or poorly lit, especially away from the main routes.

Roads also get busier during high season and around parties and festivals — if you're planning a night out, we'd strongly recommend booking a taxi rather than driving back.

Being Prepared for an Emergency

You're on holiday, and we hope you make the most of it! That said, accidents can happen from time to time, so it's worth taking a moment to look over the Emergency section of this guide so you feel prepared, just in case.

We'd also recommend keeping any information you might need in an emergency — such as your insurance details and emergency contacts — somewhere easily accessible during your stay.

A Little Thai Culture Etiquette

Thailand has a rich and welcoming culture, and a few small gestures go a long way in showing respect during your stay.

Do

  • Remove your shoes before entering temples and most homes.
  • Dress modestly when visiting temples — shoulders and knees covered.
  • Greet with a "wai" (a slight bow with palms pressed together) — it's a lovely way to show respect, though a smile and nod are always welcome too.
  • Speak softly and stay calm in public — raised voices are seen as a loss of face and can attract unwanted attention.
  • Show respect for the monarchy and images of Buddha, including on temple grounds and in everyday conversation.

Don’t

  • Avoid touching someone's head or pointing your feet at people, monks, or Buddha images — the head is considered sacred, the feet the opposite.
  • Avoid raising your voice or losing your temper in public — it rarely helps and is culturally frowned upon.
  • Keep public displays of affection low-key.
  • Never step on Thai currency — the King's image appears on it, so this is considered highly disrespectful.
  • Avoid sitting or standing higher than monks, and note that women should not touch a monk or hand him something directly.
  • Being shirtless or wearing beach attire (swimwear, bikinis) is best kept to the beach itself — please dress a little more modestly when visiting shops or other public places.

Locals genuinely appreciate the effort — nobody expects perfection, just a bit of mindfulness!

Housekeeping is included once a week. If you'd like extra cleaning during your stay, just ask — it can be arranged for 500 THB/hour, plus the cost of cleaning any additional sheets and towels used.

Please note that we don't provide extra linen between the weekly cleanings. You're welcome to request some — the associated cleaning cost will simply be added.

Towels & Pool

We provide towels for both the bathroom and the pool — the white ones are for the bathroom, and the colored ones are for the pool. Please don't take any of them to the beach, use them to clean up spills or food, or use them to remove makeup, to avoid permanent damage.

If you've been to the beach, please rinse off with a shower before using the pool — this helps keep sand out of the pool and prevents damage to the filtration system.

We kindly ask that you take care of the towels during your stay — if any come back damaged or permanently stained, we'll have to ask you to cover the cost of replacing them.

Bedding

Please be mindful not to stain the sheets with temporary or permanent tattoos, body oil, or sunscreen — these can permanently damage the bedding.

The same goes here — if the sheets end up damaged beyond cleaning, we'll kindly ask you to cover the replacement cost. We really appreciate your care!

Furniture & Sinks

The villa's indoor furniture is custom-made from valuable wood, so we kindly ask you not to move it around. In the event of damage, we'll have to ask you to cover the cost of repair or replacement.

The sinks are made of polished cement — please don't use them for laundry or other chemical products, as detergents can permanently damage both the cement and the plumbing.

Likewise, if you shave or cut your hair, please dispose of the trimmings in the bins provided rather than rinsing them down the drain, to avoid blocking the pipes. Thank you for your understanding!

Noise Disturbances

We hope you'll enjoy your stay and make the most of your time at the villa! We're located in a residential area, surrounded by neighbours who also call this place home. In the neighbourhood, there have been times when gatherings, parties or excessively loud music created disturbances, and on occasion, this led to neighbours calling the police — something we'd naturally rather avoid. We appreciate your mindfulness in this regard.

If you're ever disturbed by noise from the neighbours, particularly during quiet hours, please let us know — we'll be glad to remind them to be mindful of their noise as well.

In Case of an Accident or Major Health Issue at the Villa

If an accident or a major health issue happens at the villa, please contact the appropriate emergency services first. Alongside this, don't hesitate to reach out to us as well — we may be able to help, for instance by directing an ambulance to the villa or assisting with your care.

On the phone with a hospital

If you ever need to call a hospital or ambulance, please try to stay as calm as possible — it genuinely helps the person on the other end assist you faster. When you get through, try to clearly give, in this order:

  • Your exact location — the villa address (see "Directions") and, if useful, your phone number
  • What happened and how many people are affected
  • The person's condition — are they conscious, breathing, bleeding?
  • Stay on the line and follow their instructions until help arrives

Please also note that private hospitals in Thailand will usually ask for your travel/medical insurance details before starting treatment, so it's worth keeping your policy number and provider's contact easily accessible during your stay. Guests without valid medical insurance will kindly be referred to the public hospital (Koh Phangan Government Hospital), where care remains available to everyone.

Seasons &
Weather

Peak season — mid-December to mid-April

The best time to visit: dry, sunny days, low humidity and calm seas. Temperatures average 28–32°C. This is the most popular period on the island, with tourism running at full capacity.

Hot season — mid-April to early July

The hottest stretch of the year, with daytime temperatures often above 34°C and high humidity, plus the occasional storm. Quieter and better value, but pack light clothing and stay hydrated.

High season — July to August

Mostly sunny with scattered tropical showers. Temperatures average 30–32°C. A good balance of pleasant weather and fewer crowds.

Low / wet season — September to mid-December

The quietest and rainiest time of year, especially in October and November, with cloudier, more humid days. Temperatures stay warm at 27–30°C — rain usually comes in short bursts, and the sun often returns soon after.

Seasonal Travel Tips

Light, breathable clothing works year-round on Koh Phangan, but a few extras are worth packing depending on when you visit.

  • Peak season (mid-December to mid-April): Days are dry and sunny — sunscreen, a hat and plenty of water are your best friends. Evenings can feel slightly cooler, especially in January, so a light layer is handy for dinners out.
  • Hot season (mid-April to early July): This is the hottest stretch of the year — stick to light, loose-fitting fabrics, stay hydrated, and try to plan outdoor activities for the morning or late afternoon.
  • High season (July to August): Expect occasional short tropical showers — a light rain jacket or umbrella is worth having on hand, though the sun usually isn't far behind.
  • Low / wet season (September to mid-December): Rain is more frequent, sometimes heavier — waterproof footwear or sandals, a rain jacket, and a dry bag for electronics are all useful, especially if you're getting around by scooter.

A note on mosquitoes: dengue fever is present in Thailand, with cases tending to rise during and after the rainy season (roughly August to December). Mosquito repellent, especially around dawn and dusk, is the best precaution — the villa provides mosquito coils, and we'd recommend packing a repellent with DEET or picaridin for extra peace of mind.

If you develop a high fever, joint pain or a rash during or shortly after your stay, please see a doctor and mention possible dengue exposure.
